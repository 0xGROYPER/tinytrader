# Spread builder

An options position from a structure, or suggested for a stock picked in the Options screener, priced from the live chain and sent as an order to an account.

**Data:** a service serving option chains (alpaca-data): quotes from Databento OPRA where a relay carries the underlying (SPY, XSP), else Alpaca's indicative feed, with implied volatility computed by alpaca-data from each mid (Alpaca's own values are not used). Refreshed every 30 seconds.

**Using it:**

- Type an underlying in the ticker box, or click a ticker in another widget (Earnings week). Pick the expiry; each shows the move priced into it.
- **From the screener:** clicking a row opens the builder (beside the screener if it isn't in the workspace) with a suggested position and why (`lib/recommend.js`). Options priced rich against realized volatility (IV over realized at 1.15× or more), or a high-IV or expected-move view, suggest an iron condor: shorts at the 16th and 84th percentiles of the expiry's implied distribution, wings near the 4th and 96th. Priced cheap (0.85× or less): an at-the-money long straddle. On the Put skew view, a skew of 2 points or more either way: a credit spread on the rich side (short near the 25th or 75th percentile, long near the 8th or 92nd). The expiry is the one nearest 30 days, between 14 and 60, with a usable distribution where there is one; an earnings report before it is pointed out. Strikes sold have a bid, strikes bought an ask. It's a starting point: adjust it like any other.
- Pick a structure from the list, from `lib/structures.js`: verticals (Bull Call Debit, Bull Put Credit, Bear Put Debit, Bear Call Credit), range (iron condor, iron fly, call and put butterflies, short strangle and straddle), breakout (long straddle and strangle, reverse iron condor) and single calls or puts. Legs are placed from the at-the-money strike outward, two listed strikes apart. Edited legs keep a name when their shape has one, marked "(edited)": a Bull Call Debit with its strikes moved is still a Bull Call Debit, a call bought against two sold higher is a call ratio spread, a butterfly with one wider wing is a broken-wing butterfly, with a line on what it's for. Only a shape with no name is Custom. **+ Add spread…** beside Add leg adds a whole vertical to the legs there are (a credit spread with its short strike one width out of the money, calls above and puts below; a debit spread at the money), netting legs on the same contract: a Bull Put Credit plus a Bear Call Credit becomes an iron condor. Move its strikes as with any leg.
- The legs are a positions table like the broker's: each contract's symbol, mid price (bid × ask beneath), signed quantity (negative sells), market value and delta in shares, with the net in the last row. Step a strike with ‹ ›, switch call or put with C/P, step the quantity with − / + (through zero it flips between buy and sell; shift-click sizes the whole position by one unit, keeping its ratios), remove a leg with ×, or add one. Click a contract's symbol for its chart on [Candles](candles.md). The structure becomes Custom.
- One line sums it up per position of 100 shares a contract: the credit or debit at the mid, the risk (maximum loss), the most it can make where that isn't the credit, and the chance of profit at expiry. Hover it for the price crossing the spread, the breakevens, the delta in shares, where the chance came from and the quote source. The chance comes from the expiry's implied distribution, fitted to the whole chain with its skew and fat tails ([the method](../implied-distribution.md)), when the fit is good or rough; for a chain too thin to fit, from a lognormal at the at-the-money volatility.
- **Positions held:** an account card's **Payoff** button (Accounts) loads that account's options on one underlying and expiry as legs at their entry prices: the summary shows what was paid, what it's worth now and the P&L, and the payoff chart and chance of profit are the real position's. Moving a leg to another strike or type (or changing the expiry) makes it a what-if, priced at the market; Trade is hidden while held legs remain, since an order for them would open more.
- **Trade** opens the review: pick the account (a paper one unless you choose live), check every contract, and set the **limit** (net per spread, positive pays, negative receives; it starts at the mid as it was when you pressed Trade and stays there). It shows the most you pay or the least you receive and the maximum loss at that limit. The quantities are the legs' own (two of each leg is two spreads). **Send order** sends it to that account's order gateway as one combo order (one order for a single leg), day, opening, as the console's trade client `console` (ids `tt-`), where the gateway's caps and kill switch apply ([order gateway](../../services/order-gateway/README.md)). Changing a leg closes the review. If the account already has open orders (from anywhere, as on its [Accounts](accounts.md) card), the review lists them in red first, and **Trade** shows how many are open across the accounts. The answer is shown as the gateway gives it: accepted (not a fill), rejected, not sent, or unknown (it may have reached the broker: don't resend; it shows under working orders if it did). The console's working orders on every account are listed with **Cancel**, refreshed every 10 seconds. Without the console's trade token configured, the review says so and sends nothing.
- **The live account** (real money) is in the account list too, through its own gateway with small caps ($1,000 an order, 2 contracts a leg, 4 open orders, 20 submits a day). It's never the default: a paper account is. Its review is marked live, and **Send live order** stays disabled until you type the account's name; the console also refuses a live order the review didn't confirm. Flatten and Resume aren't offered for it.
